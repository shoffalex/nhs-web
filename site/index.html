About

What the National Honor Society is

The National Honor Society elevates a school's commitment to the values of scholarship, service, leadership, and character. NHS chapters and students are in schools that care not only about student achievement, but also community engagement. NHS students and their peers volunteer in their communities at the highest rates and make connecting with and serving within the community a priority.

A century of tradition

Unveiled over a century ago, the National Honor Society enshrines four pillars at its heart. They are more than badges of honor — they are keys that unlock potential.

More than a GPA

Grades open the door, but membership is judged on the whole student: how you serve, how you lead, and how you treat people when nobody is keeping score.

Rooted at Pine View

Our chapter meets monthly, supports school and community service, and is guided by a student board alongside advisor Ms. Robin Melton.

The four pillars

What we are judged on — and live by

These principles are transformative keys that unlock potential, enhancing every student's educational journey, and ultimately empowering them to make profound, enduring contributions to our world.

Scholarship

Scholarship is more than a report card; it represents a dedicated commitment to lifelong learning. It's about optimizing the educational opportunities offered and pursuing knowledge within and beyond academic confines, with an aspiration to contribute positively to the world through one's own knowledge, skills, and experiences.

In short: keep learning, on purpose, everywhere.

Service

Service represents the choice of stepping outside oneself to meet the needs of others. It's about performing selfless acts that transcend the ordinary, changes that resonate through our schools, ripple across our communities, and reverberate on a global scale. In the noble act of service, we sculpt a better world with our hands, our hearts, and our humanity.

In short: show up for people who need it.

Leadership

Leadership is more than a position or title; it means embodying the spirit of initiative, innovation, and influence. It's about inspiring peers through actions, nurturing a collaborative environment, and championing positive change — an interplay of intellect, empathy, and resilience, guiding others while continually learning and evolving.

In short: take initiative, bring people with you.

Character

Character is the essence of who we are. It is about valuing diversity, building relationships grounded in empathy, and exhibiting qualities like perseverance, respect, integrity, and honesty. Character lies not in public acts of virtue or visible mistakes, but in the consistent commitment to ethical and compassionate decision-making.

In short: do the right thing, consistently.

Membership requirements

Do you qualify?

Candidates are reviewed against every item below — not just the GPA. Use the checker to see where you stand.

  • Rising 11th or 12th grader You apply the spring before the year you would be a member.
  • 3.5 unweighted and 4.0 weighted GPA Both thresholds must be met — one does not substitute for the other.
  • 3 teacher recommendations Students do not seek these out. Teachers make recommendations on the document given out in February.
  • No incidents of academic dishonesty Integrity is non-negotiable under the character pillar.
  • Minimal absences and tardies Attendance records are reviewed as part of the application.
  • No disciplinary infractions A clean discipline record is expected of candidates.
  • A substantial number of community service hours Outside of school, and not all related to club activities. The specific number is not released, to avoid fraudulent applications.
  • Consistent involvement in a variety of extra-curricular activities Again, the specific number is not released, to avoid fraudulent applications.
Why some numbers are secret. The exact service-hour and activity counts are deliberately withheld so that applications reflect genuine involvement rather than a minimum target. Aim for depth and consistency, not a magic number.

How to join

The application timeline

Applications for qualified candidates for the upcoming school year are available starting February 2nd, with induction held in late April or early May.

  1. Before February

    Build the record

    Keep both GPAs above the line, log community service hours outside of school, and stay consistently involved in activities.

  2. February 2nd

    Applications become available

    Qualified candidates for the upcoming school year can pick up the application. The teacher-recommendation document is handed out in February.

  3. February – April

    Teacher recommendations

    Three teachers submit recommendations on the February document. Students do not request these themselves.

  4. Late April / early May

    Induction ceremony

    Accepted candidates are formally inducted into the Pine View chapter.

Questions

Frequently asked

How many service hours do I actually need?

The specific number is not released. This is intentional — publishing a threshold invites applications built to hit a minimum rather than reflecting real involvement. What is stated: the hours must be substantial, earned outside of school, and not all related to club activities.

Do I ask teachers for recommendations?

No. Students do not seek these out. Teachers make recommendations on the document given out in February. Three recommendations are required.

My weighted GPA is above 4.0 but my unweighted is 3.4. Do I qualify?

Both minimums must be met: 3.5 unweighted and 4.0 weighted. One does not compensate for the other. Contact Ms. Melton if you have questions about how your GPA is calculated.

When and where does the chapter meet?

Meetings are on the 2nd Tuesday of the month at 1:10 pm, in the Auditorium unless otherwise stated. The full dated schedule is on the Member Information page.

When is induction?

Induction is held in late April or early May, after applications open on February 2nd.

How do I keep up with chapter news?

Follow @nhspineview on Instagram and check Schoology. Members can also find documents on the File Share page.
